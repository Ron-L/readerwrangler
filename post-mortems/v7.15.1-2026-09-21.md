# Post-Mortem: 7.15.1 — empty the Trash from a right-click

**Date**: 2026-09-21
**Release**: 7.15.1 (app), prod+dev, 2 alphas. Right-clicking Trash now always opens a menu — "Empty
Trash (N)" when there are deleted books, a muted "Trash is empty" when not. A "quick win" off the TODO.
mobile.js untouched.

Small feature, but the process story is worth keeping: it was the **first real use of the NEVER-ASSUME
rule** codified minutes earlier, and the rule paid for itself immediately.

## The NEVER-ASSUME payoff
The TODO item (which I had written) asserted: *"the Trash row likely just isn't wired with an
onContextMenu — wire it."* Instead of acting on that, I grepped and read the source first. The row was
**already wired** ([js 15682]) — but the handler was **gated on `trashCount > 0`**, so an empty-Trash
right-click did nothing. Had I trusted my own TODO's premise, I'd have added a redundant/incorrect
wire and never found the real cause (the gate). The rule turned a mis-premised "quick win" into the
*actual* fix (always open; show state when empty) on the first try. The word-tell fired on my own note:
"likely."

## ✅ What worked
1. **Verified the premise, didn't inherit it.** Even a TODO *I* wrote is a point-in-time assumption;
   the code is the truth. (Law 2 / NEVER ASSUME, one release after it bit us in 7.15.0.)
2. **Named my own scope creep instead of sneaking it.** I reflexively wrote "Empty Trash (N)" with a
   count — an unrequested change — caught it, reverted to plain "Empty Trash", and *offered* the count.
   Ron opted in. Scope stays a visible decision, not a silent addition (the 7.15.0 `.fixed` lesson,
   applied).
3. **UX call held to Law 16.** Chose a muted "Trash is empty" *status row* over a greyed-out "Empty
   Trash" *command* — state the state, don't dress a display as a dead control. Ron left the call to me
   and it matched the intent.
4. **CHANGELOG word before push, even under "push to both".** Confirmed the copy Ron hadn't seen rather
   than assuming his "word" covered it — his own over-confirm trade, applied same-day.

## ❌ What went wrong
- The TODO note shipped a wrong premise ("isn't wired") in the first place — written from a quick read,
  not a verified one. Low cost here (caught at pickup), but a reminder that **filed assumptions age into
  false facts**; a TODO should say what was *observed*, not what's *assumed*.

## 👀 Watch

## Final thought
The feature is a two-line conditional. The point of the entry is that "NEVER ASSUME" stopped being a
slogan the moment it was written down: the very next task carried a plausible-but-wrong premise, and
reading the source instead of trusting it produced a better fix with less code. The mechanism worked on
its first at-bat.
